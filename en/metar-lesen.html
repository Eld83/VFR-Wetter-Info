Reading METAR

The METAR (Meteorological Aerodrome Report) is an airport's current weather report – short, standardized, and updated every 30 to 60 minutes. If you fly VFR, being able to read a raw METAR matters, even though an app like VFR Wetter translates it into plain language automatically. This article walks through the structure group by group using a fully worked example.

A sample METAR

A report for Frankfurt might look like this:

EDDF 151050Z 24012G22KT 210V270 9999 FEW035 SCT250 22/14 Q1015 NOSIG

It looks cryptic at first – but broken into its groups, it paints a clear picture of the weather situation.

Station and time

EDDF is the airport's ICAO code (here, Frankfurt). 151050Z means: report from the 15th of the month, 10:50 UTC (the "Z" stands for Zulu time, i.e. UTC). The report time is always UTC, never local time – mixing the two up is a common beginner mistake.

Wind

24012G22KT: wind from 240°, 12 knots, with gusts (G) up to 22 knots. 210V270 shows the direction varying between 210° and 270° (only reported when the direction is sufficiently variable). Calm wind is shown as 00000KT; a strongly shifting direction with no clear trend is reported as VRB instead of a fixed heading.

Visibility

9999 represents a horizontal visibility of 10 km or more (the maximum reporting step). Below that, visibility is given in meters, e.g. 3000 for 3,000 m. If runway visual range is also available, it follows as its own group, such as R25/0800.

Weather phenomena

If present, a group of intensity, descriptor and phenomenon follows, e.g. -RA for light rain, +TSRA for a strong thunderstorm with rain, or BR for mist. Our example has no such group – there's simply no reportable weather phenomenon.

Clouds

FEW035: a few clouds (1–2 oktas) at 3,500 ft. SCT250: scattered cloud (3–4 oktas) at 25,000 ft. The steps are FEW (1–2/8), SCT (3–4/8), BKN (5–7/8) and OVC (8/8, an overcast layer). BKN or OVC is what's called a ceiling. If there's no cloud group at all, you'll see NSC (no significant cloud) instead, or CAVOK if visibility and weather also clear their respective thresholds.

Temperature and dew point

22/14: 22°C air temperature, 14°C dew point. Values below 0°C get a leading M (e.g. M02 for −2°C). The closer temperature and dew point sit together, the higher the tendency toward fog or condensation.

Pressure

Q1015 is the QNH in hectopascals – the sea-level-reduced pressure you set on your altimeter so it correctly reads elevation above mean sea level. In the US and some other countries, an A is used instead, followed by the pressure in inches of mercury, e.g. A2992.

Trend

NOSIG ("no significant change") means no significant change is expected over the next two hours. Alternatively, BECMG (gradual change) or TEMPO (temporary fluctuation) can follow, each with its own short weather group.

Common abbreviations at a glance

  • Cloud cover: FEW (1–2/8) · SCT (3–4/8) · BKN (5–7/8) · OVC (8/8) · NSC/SKC (none/clear) · VV (vertical visibility when the sky is obscured)
  • Intensity: − light · (no sign) moderate · + heavy
  • Common phenomena: RA rain · SN snow · BR mist · FG fog · TS thunderstorm · SH shower · GR hail
  • Other: AUTO automated report · CAVOK visibility/cloud/weather all above thresholds · SPECI unscheduled report for a sudden change
