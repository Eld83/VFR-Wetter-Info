VFR minima by airspace class

By Niels Deuter, developer of VFR Wetter · updated 15 Aug 2026

Important note upfront: This article gives an orientation-level overview of the structure of VMC minimum conditions under SERA.5001. It does not replace legal advice or a check of the current, applicable version. Only SERA in its current version and your country's AIP and NOTAM/AIC notices are legally binding – for national exemptions (such as in Germany), always check the current official source.

VMC stands for Visual Meteorological Conditions – the weather conditions under which VFR flight is permitted at all in a given airspace. Unlike the MVFR/IFR/LIFR weather categories, this isn't a rule-of-thumb classification but a Europe-wide legal rule: SERA.5001 (Standardised European Rules of the Air).

The basic SERA structure

Minimum flight visibility and distance from cloud depend on two factors: altitude and airspace class. Roughly simplified:

  • At and above FL100 (10,000 ft AMSL): flight visibility at least 8 km, cloud clearance at least 1,500 m horizontally and 300 m (1,000 ft) vertically.
  • Below FL100, but above 900 m (3,000 ft) AMSL or 300 m (1,000 ft) above terrain (whichever is higher): flight visibility at least 5 km, same cloud clearance as above.
  • At or below 900 m (3,000 ft) AMSL or 300 m (1,000 ft) above terrain: in controlled airspace classes B–E, generally clear of cloud and in sight of the surface, flight visibility at least 5 km; the same base rule applies in uncontrolled classes F and G, where SERA allows member states defined exemptions.

Germany's specific rule for airspace F/G

Germany makes use of exactly that exemption: for aircraft other than helicopters flying at 140 kt IAS or less, in Class F and G airspace below the altitude limit mentioned above, by day, a reduced flight visibility of 1,500 m can be sufficient, provided there's a reasonable opportunity to see other traffic and obstacles in time. This is one reason small, slow VFR aircraft can be legally flying in uncontrolled German airspace even in moderate visibility, where a faster aircraft would already be at the minimum. The exact, currently valid wording is in the German AIP.

Why this shouldn't be confused with the weather category

A METAR category of "VFR" (ceiling > 3,000 ft, visibility > 5 SM) says nothing about whether VFR flight is legally permitted in the specific airspace at that location – that also depends on airspace class and altitude. Conversely, "MVFR" weather in Class F/G airspace below 3,000 ft can still be legally VFR-suitable under Germany's 1,500 m exemption. The two systems answer different questions: weather category = how good is the weather in general, VMC minima = is VFR flight legally permitted here and now.

Frequently asked questions

What is SERA?

Standardised European Rules of the Air – the EU-wide rules of the air (Implementing Regulation (EU) No 923/2012). Among other things, it sets the VMC minimum conditions for visibility and cloud clearance.

Why does Germany sometimes allow lower visibility than the base SERA table?

SERA allows member states defined exemptions, such as reduced flight visibility in Class F and G airspace below certain altitudes for aircraft at 140 kt IAS or less, by day. Germany makes use of this – the exact, currently valid wording is in the German AIP and relevant NfL notices.

Is the MVFR/IFR/LIFR category the same as VFR minima?

No. Those weather categories are a practical rule-of-thumb classification, not a legal basis. Whether VFR flight is permitted is determined exclusively by the official minima under SERA and the applicable national regulations.
